Kindle vs Kobo: Which E-Reader Fits Your Book Library?

Kindle and Kobo disagree on the one thing that decides everything: which files they open. Here is the format difference, the library and DRM mechanics, and the exact conversion bridges if you ever switch sides.

October 22, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • •The single biggest difference is file format: Kobo opens EPUB natively, while a Kindle only accepts EPUB through Amazon Send to Kindle, which converts the file on Amazon servers before delivery.
  • •Sideloading is simpler on Kobo: copy a DRM-free EPUB over USB and it opens exactly as-is. On Kindle, the dependable sideload target is AZW3, which you produce by converting the EPUB yourself.
  • •Purchased books are locked to their store: Kindle Store titles (AZW3/KFX with Amazon DRM) do not open on Kobo, and Kobo store titles (EPUB with Adobe DRM) do not open on Kindle.
  • •Both handle library loans, but differently: Kobo has OverDrive/Libby built into the device (supported regions), while Kindle receives library loans through the Libby app where supported (mainly US/UK).
  • •Switching ecosystems is a one-time conversion job for DRM-free files: AZW3 or MOBI to EPUB for a Kobo, EPUB to AZW3 for a Kindle sideload.

Kindle or Kobo is not really a hardware question — screens from both camps are pleasant to read on, and neither brand has a meaningful display monopoly. The decision is made by your files. The two ecosystems disagree on which ebook formats they open natively, how purchases are protected, and how library loans arrive, and those three differences decide your daily routine more than any spec sheet. This guide walks through the format landscape first, then the practical routes for getting your existing library onto each device, and finally the conversion bridges that make switching between the two far less painful than it sounds.

The core difference in one sentence

Kobo opens EPUB natively. Kindle does not.

That is the whole split in one line. EPUB is the open standard used by almost every ebook store outside Amazon, so a Kobo treats the most common ebook file as its home format: you copy one over USB and it simply opens. A Kindle has no EPUB reader at all. The only way EPUB reaches a Kindle is through Amazon Send to Kindle, where the file is uploaded and converted on Amazon servers before delivery — or you convert it yourself to AZW3, the Kindle-native format, and copy it over.

Neither approach is wrong, but they create very different habits. On Kobo, your library stays in one universal format. On Kindle, your files either pass through Amazon conversion pipeline or you maintain AZW3 copies. What the Kindle formats actually are — AZW3, KFX, and the retired MOBI — is laid out in our Kindle formats guide.

Sideloading: getting your own files onto each device

Sideloading means moving files you already own onto the reader over USB, without a store in the middle.

On Kobo: connect over USB and drag DRM-free EPUB or PDF files into the device storage. The file you copied is the file that opens — no conversion step, no server round-trip. This is the most direct sideload experience of the two.

On Kindle: you have two routes. Send to Kindle accepts the EPUB directly, but Amazon converts it on their servers and the result sometimes flattens complex layouts, tables, and embedded fonts. The full-control route is to convert the EPUB to AZW3 yourself, verify the output, then copy it to the device: the walkthrough is in EPUB to AZW3 for Kindle, and the delivery-route trade-offs are compared in How to Send EPUB to Kindle.

The summary: Kobo sideloading is drag-and-drop; Kindle sideloading is drag-and-drop after one conversion step. For plain novels the difference is negligible. For cookbooks, textbooks, and styled books, the self-managed AZW3 conversion is actually the higher-fidelity option on either device.

Library books and DRM

Both ecosystems support borrowing library ebooks, but the mechanics differ enough to matter for heavy borrowers.

Kobo has OverDrive/Libby integrated into the device itself (in supported regions: US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and several others): you browse your library collection, sign in, and borrow straight from the reader. The loan arrives as an Adobe-DRM EPUB that the device is authorized to open.

Kindle receives library loans through the Libby app on your phone (where supported — primarily the US and UK): borrow in Libby, choose Kindle as the delivery target, and Amazon delivers a Kindle-format copy to your device. The protected EPUB itself never touches the Kindle — Amazon handles the conversion of the loan through its own channel. The mechanics are described in How to Read EPUB on Kindle.

One more thing to be honest about: DRM cuts in both directions. Books bought from the Kobo store carry Adobe DRM and open only on Kobo-authorized devices or apps, just as Kindle Store books open only within the Amazon world. The freedom to move between devices belongs to DRM-free files — which is also what conversion tools can legally process.

Ecosystem: what each store locks in

Kindle is built around the Amazon store: the largest ebook catalog, Kindle Unlimited subscription reading, Whispersync for position syncing, and X-Ray. Books you buy there arrive as AZW3/KFX with Amazon DRM. That library is a real asset — and a real commitment. It lives comfortably on Kindles, the Kindle app on phones and tablets, and nowhere else.

Kobo is owned by Rakuten and runs its own store with a comparable catalog in most regions, plus Kobo Plus, a subscription for ebooks and audiobooks. Kobo store purchases are EPUB files under Adobe DRM — the more portable protection scheme, but a lock nonetheless.

The decision rule that survives contact with reality: look at where your money already went. If your library is mostly Kindle purchases, a Kobo does not read any of it. If your library is mostly EPUB from non-Amazon stores, a Kindle cannot open any of it natively. Hardware preferences rarely outweigh several hundred already-purchased books.

Switching between Kindle and Kobo: the conversion bridges

Switching sides — or keeping a device from each camp — is a one-time job for DRM-free files. The bridges:

Kindle to Kobo. Your AZW3 and MOBI files need to become EPUB. AZW3 to EPUB converts with formatting preserved — chapters, cover, embedded fonts, and styles carry over, and the guide AZW3 to EPUB: Remove Kindle Lock-in walks the full path. Old MOBI files modernize the same way through MOBI to EPUB. Kobo store purchases themselves cannot be converted — Adobe DRM.

Kobo to Kindle. An EPUB has two destinations. Send to Kindle takes it directly and converts server-side — fine for text-first books. For styled books, convert EPUB to AZW3 first and copy over USB; the file you verified is what the device opens. For legacy Kindle hardware that predates AZW3, the target is MOBI instead.

Which Kindle books can be converted? Only DRM-free ones. Amazon purchases are DRM-wrapped by default and stay in the Kindle world; your own files and DRM-free purchases travel freely. A pre-flight checklist for spotting the difference is in the DRM-free checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Kobo read EPUB files natively?

Yes. EPUB is Kobo's native format: copy a DRM-free EPUB over USB and it opens exactly as-is, no conversion or server-side processing. This is the single biggest practical difference from Kindle, which has no EPUB reader and only accepts EPUB through Amazon's Send to Kindle conversion.

Can I read my Kindle books on a Kobo?

Only the DRM-free ones. Kindle Store purchases are DRM-protected AZW3/KFX files that will not open on Kobo. Your own AZW3 or MOBI files convert to EPUB — for example with BookConv's free AZW3 to EPUB tool — and then work on any Kobo.

Can I read Kobo EPUB books on my Kindle?

DRM-free EPUBs, yes: send them through Amazon Send to Kindle, or convert EPUB to AZW3 first and copy over USB for full control over formatting. Kobo store purchases carry Adobe DRM and stay within the Kobo ecosystem.

Which is better for library ebooks, Kindle or Kobo?

Both work, differently. Kobo has OverDrive built into the device, so you browse and borrow directly on the reader. Kindle receives library loans through the Libby app, where supported, and Amazon delivers them in a Kindle format. Heavy borrowers usually prefer having borrowing on-device.

Is sideloading easier on Kindle or Kobo?

On Kobo — drag a DRM-free EPUB over USB and it opens as-is. On Kindle, the dependable sideload target is AZW3, so EPUB files need one self-managed conversion step first. For styled books, that conversion step actually produces the more faithful result on the device.

Do Kindle Unlimited books work on Kobo?

No. Kindle Unlimited titles are DRM-protected and tied to the Amazon subscription, so they open only on Kindles and in the Kindle app. The equivalent on the Kobo side is Kobo Plus, which is likewise locked to Kobo devices and apps.

What formats do Kindle and Kobo each support?

Kindle: AZW3 natively, KFX for store purchases, MOBI for legacy sideloading (existing files only), PDF, and EPUB only via Send to Kindle. Kobo: EPUB and PDF natively, plus CBZ/CBR comics and MOBI on most current models.
