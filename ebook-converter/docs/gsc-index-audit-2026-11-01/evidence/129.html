How to Send an EPUB to Your Kindle: Every Delivery Route Compared

You know the Kindle needs a Kindle format — what you actually need now is the delivery step. Here is every route an EPUB can take to reach your Kindle, what each one does to your file behind the scenes, and which failures silently swallow books.

Sending an EPUB to a Kindle sounds like a one-step task until the book never shows up, arrives without its formatting, or bounces out of your outbox with no explanation. The reason is that "send" actually hides four different delivery routes, and each one handles your file differently. This guide covers the mechanics: which route to use for which situation, the settings that must be right before you press send, and the failure modes that make books vanish. If you are still deciding between the reading methods themselves, start with How to Read EPUB on Kindle — this page picks up where that one ends, at the moment of delivery.

What is Send to Kindle?

Send to Kindle is Amazon's official delivery service: you hand it a document or ebook, and Amazon converts it and delivers the finished book to your Kindle library or to a specific device linked to your account. It is not a separate purchase from Amazon — it is the built-in "send" layer behind the web uploader, the Kindle mobile and desktop apps, and the email-to-Kindle address.

The service runs at amazon.com/sendtokindle and accepts 13 file types: PDF, DOC, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTM, HTML, PNG, GIF, JPG, JPEG, BMP, and EPUB, with a 200 MB ceiling per file. Everything on this list is converted on Amazon's servers before it reaches the device — including EPUB, which is why a sent book is never your original file. Two restrictions apply across the board: files must be DRM-free, and Amazon's conversion favors straight text over complex layouts.

That combination — wide format support, generous size cap, server-side conversion — is exactly why Send to Kindle is the right default for novels and documents, and exactly why styled or layout-sensitive books need the USB route described below.

Route 1 and 2: Send to Kindle via web or app

The web uploader and the apps behave the same way: you hand over an EPUB, Amazon converts it server-side, and the finished book appears on every device registered to your account. The app route is the most forgiving — drag the file into the mobile or desktop app, pick the target device, done.

Two details decide whether this route works for you. First, only DRM-free EPUBs are accepted; a library loan will be silently dropped rather than rejected with a helpful message. Second, Amazon's conversion is optimized for straight text. Cookbooks, textbooks, multi-column layouts, custom embedded fonts, and footnote-heavy academic books come out flattened more often than not. If your book fits that description, skip ahead to the USB route.

Route 3: Email delivery and the approved-sender trap

Every Kindle has its own address (visible in Manage Your Content and Devices under your Amazon account). Emailing a DRM-free EPUB attachment to that address delivers it like the app routes do — with one extra gate that trips up almost everyone at least once.

The sender address must be on your Approved Personal Document E-mail List. Amazon does not reply to unapproved senders; the email simply goes nowhere, and you will not receive a bounce. Before your first email delivery, add the address you are sending from in your Amazon device settings.

Email is also the route with the tightest attachment limits, because it passes through a mail gateway rather than a direct upload. Newsletters, short documents, and small novels travel fine; a 40 MB illustrated EPUB is better served by another route. A practical habit: email is for files that originate somewhere other than your current device — everything else moves faster through the app.

Route 4: USB copy after converting to AZW3 (full control)

This is the only route where nothing happens on Amazon's servers. You convert the EPUB to AZW3 yourself, verify the output, and copy the finished file to the Kindle's documents folder over USB. What you checked is what the device opens.

When this route is worth the extra minute:

  • Styled books. Embedded fonts, CSS, tables, and multi-level footnotes survive an AZW3 conversion you control; Amazon's pipeline frequently strips them.

  • Oversized files. EPUBs above the app routes' comfortable size range stop failing once the conversion happens locally — a 4 MB-class EPUB converts cleanly with BookConv's EPUB to AZW3 tool in the browser, no install needed.

  • Verification before delivery. You can open the AZW3 in a desktop reader, confirm the chapter order and cover, and only then move it to the device.


The same logic applies to very old Kindle hardware (roughly pre-2012) that predates AZW3 — convert to MOBI instead, using the same workflow with the EPUB to MOBI converter. What AZW3 preserves and why it is the default sideload target is explained in our AZW3 format page.

Pre-flight check: DRM and file size

Two file properties decide which routes are even open to you. Run this check before sending anything:

  • DRM status. Open the EPUB in any non-Kindle reader. If it opens, it is DRM-free and every route accepts it. If it only opens inside the Kindle app while signed in to Amazon, it is DRM-wrapped: Amazon's routes will drop it, and no converter will process it. Library loans belong to this category — deliver them through the library app's built-in Kindle delivery instead of any manual route. The full decision procedure is in our DRM-free checklist.

  • Size and complexity. A text-only novel under a few megabytes travels happily through every route. Large or image-heavy EPUBs are where the Amazon routes start failing or flattening — that is the signal to convert first and go by USB.

Troubleshooting: the book never arrived

Work down this list — it covers the failure modes in the order they actually occur:

  • Sent by email, nothing happened, no bounce. The sender is not on your Approved Personal Document E-mail List. This is the single most common silent failure. Fix the list, resend.

  • Sent to the wrong address. Each registered Kindle has its own address; old devices you have replaced keep their addresses in your account. Confirm you are sending to the address of the device you actually read on.

  • Book appears but chapters or fonts are wrong. Amazon's conversion flattened it. Re-do the conversion yourself with EPUB to AZW3 and transfer by USB.

  • Book appears on one device only. Delivery targets a single device address. The app and web routes let you pick; a book you want everywhere needs to be sent again per device, or archived to your Amazon library after delivery.

  • DRM rejection with no message. Protected files are dropped without notification on every Amazon route. Confirm DRM status with the pre-flight check above.


For books that arrive damaged rather than missing, the repair path is always the same: take the conversion back from Amazon's pipeline by converting the original EPUB locally, then redeliver the finished file.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Amazon Send to Kindle?

Send to Kindle is Amazon's official service for delivering documents and ebooks to your Kindle library or a linked device. It runs at amazon.com/sendtokindle and is also built into the Kindle mobile and desktop apps and the email-to-Kindle route. It accepts 13 file types including EPUB and PDF, up to 200 MB per file, and converts everything on Amazon's servers before delivery.

What file types does Send to Kindle support?

Send to Kindle accepts PDF, DOC, DOCX, TXT, RTF, HTM, HTML, PNG, GIF, JPG, JPEG, BMP, and EPUB. All of them are converted to a Kindle format on Amazon's servers, files must be DRM-free, and complex layouts such as tables and custom fonts often get flattened — convert styled books to AZW3 yourself and copy them over USB instead.

Why did my emailed EPUB never arrive on the Kindle?

Almost always because the sending address is not on your Approved Personal Document E-mail List in Amazon settings. Unapproved senders are dropped silently with no bounce message. Add the address, resend, and delivery usually completes within a few minutes.

Does Send to Kindle change my EPUB file?

Yes. Amazon converts the EPUB to a Kindle format on their servers and delivers that converted copy; your original EPUB never lands on the device. Simple novels survive this fine, but custom fonts, tables, and complex layouts are frequently flattened.

Can I avoid Amazon converting my file?

Only by converting the EPUB to AZW3 yourself and copying the finished file to the Kindle over USB. That is the one route where the file arrives exactly as you prepared and verified it.

What size EPUB can I send to Kindle?

The app and web routes comfortably handle standard novels; the email route faces tighter attachment limits because it passes through a mail gateway. Large or image-heavy EPUBs are more reliable after a local EPUB to AZW3 conversion, which also sidesteps any upload cap entirely.

Can I send a library EPUB book to my Kindle?

Not directly — library loans are DRM-protected and every route rejects them, usually silently. Use the library app (such as Libby) and choose Kindle as the delivery target; Amazon handles the transfer of the protected loan through its own channel.
