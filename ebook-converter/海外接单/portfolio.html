A 16.5× spread between long-form guides and programmatic pages — measured with real denominators, not anecdotes. Stated weakness: guide topics skew toward how-and-why questions, which may naturally match AI query intent. Correlation with a clean counter-example, not a proven cause. I'd rather tell you than have you find it.

Same confounding rule, applied to the 94.3% number too. The 28 long-form pages that produced 94.3% of all citations are simultaneously long AND How/Why-topical — length and topic mix are tangled, and I have not isolated them with a controlled test. Read the 94.3% as long + How/Why, correlated with citation, not long-form causes citation. Same disclosure rule as the table above.

How you can trust the numbers

Three habits, applied to my own numbers first.

  • Conservative figures. I report 1,314 citations from the top-page report, not the 1,530 in the full-window overview. I would rather understate.
  • The bad numbers, published. Total organic clicks on the lab site in six weeks: 19. Google AI visibility: 16 impressions while Bing cited me 1,314 times. Both channels, both directions, no cherry-picking.
  • I overturn my own conclusions. Two findings I published on this project were reversed by later full-population data, and both reversals are documented. A conclusion you cannot falsify is not worth paying for.
Measurement first. Then diagnosis. Then I try to prove myself wrong before I tell you what to do.
