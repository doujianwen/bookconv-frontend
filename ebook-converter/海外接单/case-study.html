Case study · Measured, not estimated

What 41 days of AI search visibility actually looks like — including the part that went wrong

I built an ebook conversion site as a live lab. In its first six weeks it earned 1,314 AI citations across 31 pages in Bing and Copilot — and lost 88% of its Google impressions to the August spam update. Both results point the same direction.

The finding that matters

I split the 31 AI-cited pages by page type and divided by the real number of published pages in each type. That denominator is what makes this a finding instead of an anecdote.

guide 54.5%, blog 27.6%, programmatic 3.3%.

Citation rate = pages that received at least one AI citation ÷ published pages of that type. Denominators counted from source: 22 guides, 58 blog posts, 30 programmatic pages.

A 16.5× spread. 28 long-form pages produced 94.3% of all AI citations. 30 programmatic pages produced 0.8%. An independent measurement on the same site — zero-citation rate by type — points the same way: 90% of programmatic pages never got cited at all, versus 20% of guides.

Same confounding rule, applied to the 94.3% number too. Length and How/Why topic mix are tangled. I have not isolated page length from topic type with a controlled test. Read the 94.3% citation share as long + How/Why, correlated with citation, not long-form causes citation. Same disclosure rule as the warning box above.

The weakness, stated first. This is a correlation, not a proven cause. Guide topics skew toward how-and-why questions, which may naturally match AI query intent better than format-conversion pages do. I have not run a controlled test on that. I would rather tell you than have you find it.

How the attribution worked

  • Ruled out demand change first. Impressions per query stayed flat at 1.14–1.80 before and after, inside the same band as the healthy period. The loss came from the number of queries getting impressions, not from ranking decay on existing ones.
  • Found the two-peak split. Homepage retained 79.6% of its impressions (86.2% day-normalized). Programmatic pages retained 8.2%. Blog 3.3%. That bimodal shape — not a uniform decline — is what points at scaled content rather than a sitewide quality signal.
  • Caught the second signal on the same day. New keyword acquisition fell from +15–17/day to +1/day on August 22, the same day impressions collapsed. Broad match was withdrawn, so new queries stopped being served.
  • Did not panic-edit. No deletions, no mass merges, no redirects for 14 days. I set a re-test date instead.

Confidence, honestly. I attribute the drop to the August 18 spam update hitting scaled content. That is inference, not an official Google statement about this property — no console message, no manual action. I would call it medium-high confidence, and I will say so to a client rather than dress it up.

What I will not claim

This is a visibility case study, not a traffic case study. Total organic clicks across both engines in six weeks: 19 (6 from Google, 13 from Bing). I put that number in my own sales material because a client will find it in thirty seconds of shared screen, and I would rather be the one who says it.

I cannot explain the August 25 step. Citations went from 38 to 147 in one day. No action of mine accounts for it. I will not tell you my method caused it.

Citation counts are reported conservatively. I use 1,314 from the top-page report, not the 1,530 in the full-window overview. The difference is a reporting-window artifact, and I would rather understate.

My Google AI visibility is near zero. Sixteen impressions in six weeks, on five pages, and nothing since August 21. If your buyers search on Google's AI surfaces, I will measure that for you honestly — but I will not promise you a result I have not produced on my own site.
